Real CSS, not a mock-up: the pane blurs whatever is behind it the same way the dashboard does. The names and numbers are the demo day's fictional data.

Behind the glass what the blur has to work with

Glass is only visible as the difference between sharp and blurred. The shipped backdrop was blurred and flattened to one blue before the pane blurred it again, so there is no difference left to see.

The pane fill, blur, size

72%Shipped at 72%, more than any glass the research found (the highest was 60%; most panes run 10 to 30%).
28 px18 to 25 px reads as frosted. It only shows if there is something sharp behind the pane.
1.25Boosting colour under the blur is what keeps a frosted pane from going grey.
4%A pane needs room around it to be seen standing in a space.
24 px

The edge where glass announces itself

A bright top edge and a shaded bottom edge, one pixel each. This is the single cheapest cue that a surface is a solid.
A soft diagonal highlight across the surface, the reflection of a light source.
Lifts the pane off the backdrop. Shipped has one.
Shipped has a 40 px white glow inside the edge. It fogs the pane; the recipe drops it.
A faint noise texture, the frost itself.
An SVG displacement filter bends the backdrop near the edge the way Apple's Liquid Glass does. Safari and Firefox ignore it.

The cards glass on glass

72%Shipped: 72% white cards on a 72% pane. The recipe keeps cards flat at about 60% so the text is easy, and lets the pane be the glass.
Does nothing, on purpose: a card inside a blurred pane can only see the pane, never the room (finding 3 below). The shipped cards carry this dead setting.
16 px

The type Figtree, the dashboard's face

The dashboard sets -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased, which thins every letter. On a 1080p monitor that is most of "the fonts look poor". No visible change on a Retina screen.
