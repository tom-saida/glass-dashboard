What the research found

Tom, you are right, and it is not one mistake but several that stack. (1) The pane is 72% white. That is more opaque than any glass I found in any source (the highest was 60%; ordinary glass panes run 10-30%), so it hides the room instead of showing it. (2) The room behind it is already blurred and almost a single blue, so there is nothing sharp for the glass to soften: the pane's 28px blur changes the photo by about 5 brightness levels out of 255, and after the 72% fill only about 1.5 of those survive. (3) The cards inside the pane carry their own glass setting, and it does nothing. A browser rule says anything inside an element that has backdrop-filter cannot see the page behind it, so the cards are just more white paper. I reproduced that in Chromium. (4) The pane covers 82% of the window and has a thick white frame, so it reads as an app window with a pale skin, not a slab of glass standing in a room. Glass reads as real when you can see something sharp and colourful cross its edge and turn soft, when the pane is mostly see-through (fill roughly 10-30%), and when the edge catches light from one side like a thin bevel. Apple's name for the part we are missing is lensing, light bending at the edge. Plain CSS cannot do lensing. Chrome can fake it with an SVG filter, and Safari cannot as of today (the WebKit fix is still unmerged on 2026-11-02). So the honest target is convincing frosted glass in every browser, with true edge refraction as a Chrome-only extra on the corner objects. The fonts are a separate cause: the page sets -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased (it comes from the Vibe component library), which makes Chrome on a Mac draw text about 12% lighter, and the text is mostly 12-14px. Both are fixable below, but nobody has yet rendered the fix on Tom's own 1080p screen, so treat the numbers as a tested starting point, not a finished design.

Four research angles (Apple's Liquid Glass and its web copies, glassmorphism fundamentals, the property stacks working designers use, typography and text rendering), each re-checked by a skeptic who reopened every source: 100 of 125 cited claims confirmed. A fifth agent audited the dashboard's own stylesheet and screenshots against the recipe. Only confirmed claims are used below.

What is wrong, in order of how much it matters

1

The pane is 72% white, so it hides the room instead of showing it

A fill of rgba(238,245,255,.72) lets only 28% of what is behind it through. Measured at the left edge of the pane on a 1920x1080 screen, the visible edge is made by the fill (brightness jumps 59-101 levels) and not by the blur (0-9 levels), so the eye reads a pale card laid on a photo. The highest fill I found in any source is 60% (Comeau's thicker-glass demo, kube.io's music player). Ordinary glass panes run 10-30%: Malewicz's generator 15%, Setproduct 12%, web.dev and NN/g 30%, KeyShot 10%, and liquid-glass-react uses no tint at all. The tint is also pre-blued (238,245,255), so the little that gets through is pushed toward the same pale blue. Apple does make glass more opaque for text-heavy parts, which is why text belongs on cards and not on bare glass.

What to change. Pane fill = diagonal white gradient .34 to .14 to .26 (average about .24), neutral white, with blur(18px) saturate(1.6) brightness(1.04). Put all text on flat cards at .58-.62 white so legibility does not depend on the pane. Full numbers in recipe_css.
Sources: web.dev · nngroup.com · joshwcomeau.com · hype4.academy · setproduct.com · kube.io · github.com · keyshot.com · developer.apple.com
2

The room behind the glass has nothing in it to blur or bend

scene.webp is 1664x1024 (so it is also upscaled on a 1920 screen), already Gaussian-blurred, and squeezed into one hue band (201-220 degrees) and one narrow brightness range. Re-measured: blur(28px) removes about 5 of 255 brightness levels from it, and after the 72% fill only about 1.5 remain, which is invisible. Malewicz, whose article started the style, says the backdrop 'can't be too simple or dull, or the effect won't be visible' and needs clear tonal differences. Apple's guidance limits the clear, see-through variant to visually rich backgrounds. Fairness note: this is a partner to cause 1, not the only cause. Over the same photo a thin-fill pane already shows windows and light shapes, and Tom's own reel sits on a soft blue office, so fix both. In my renders, either fix alone looked more like glass than the shipped build, and both together looked clearly best.

What to change. Stop pre-blurring and pre-grading. Use a sharp backdrop with at least three hue families, a dark area and a bright area, and 3-5 hard-edged shapes that cross the pane's edge with 40-60% of each shape under the pane. See the backdrop field for the build.
Sources: hype4.academy · developer.apple.com · nngroup.com · joshwcomeau.com
3

The cards' own glass is dead code, and the entrance animation fades the pane's glass

By the CSS Filter Effects spec, an element with backdrop-filter becomes a 'backdrop root', and any backdrop-filter inside it can only see what is painted inside it. Card.module.css puts blur(16px) on cards that sit inside the pane, which has its own blur. In headless Chromium 153 and 156 a child's blur under a backdrop-filter parent stayed pixel-sharp, and with the child's filter present the card ended up whiter, not more glassy. Counting the fills, only .28 x .28 = 7.8% of the room can reach a card. The variable --glass-card-blur is used, but it has no effect. The same rule bites in a second place: an ancestor with opacity below 1 removed all the blur in my test (opacity .97), and the pane's 900ms entrance runs opacity .5 to 1, so the glass is faint while it plays. A WebKit engineer confirmed the same rule for Safari. Apple also says never to put glass on glass.

What to change. Delete backdrop-filter from .card. Give cards a flat fill (.58-.62 white). Put the pane's backdrop-filter on its own empty layer that is a sibling of the content, with no ancestor that has opacity below 1, filter, mask, clip-path, mix-blend-mode or will-change. Animate the entrance with transform only and end at transform: none.
Sources: drafts.csswg.org · developer.mozilla.org · bugs.webkit.org · meta.discourse.org · developer.apple.com
4

The pane fills the window, so it reads as an app window, not a glass object in a room

At 1920x1080 the pane is 1792x952, which is 82% of the screen; the room shows in 17.7% of the area. In the audit's measurement the cards cover about 85% of the pane, so the glass itself shows only in the margin strip and the gaps. Apple's guidance is that glass is a floating layer for controls and navigation above content, not the content layer, and Microsoft says not to put acrylic on large background surfaces. Both are about this exact pattern. That is what Tom means by 'it just sits in the browser'.

What to change. Inset 88px at 1920x1080 (pane becomes 76% of the screen, with room visible all round), corner radius 44px (Tom's second reference is about 48px), 24px gutters between cards so the glass shows between them, and concentric card radius = pane radius minus padding (20px). Keep it ONE pane: Setproduct says two stacked glass panels usually look worse, and Microsoft warns that side-by-side panes leave a visible seam. The rail gets a flat fill, not its own glass.
Sources: developer.apple.com · learn.microsoft.com · setproduct.com · hype4.academy · 30secondsofcode.org
5

The edge is a thick white frame with no thickness cues, and true lensing is missing

Apple says lensing is the primary way Liquid Glass defines itself, and that bigger glass is simulated as thicker material with deeper shadows and stronger bending. Ours stacks a 1.5px .88-white border, a 4px gradient ring that never fades out on the unlit sides (it is already directional, so 'uniform' was wrong, but it is 4px thick), a 6px band, a 7px blue line and a 40px inner glow that sits over real content. That reads as a picture frame. The outer drop shadow (0 40px 90px, .45) is present and heavy, so shadow is not the problem. The implementations that look right use a 1-1.5px lit edge that fades to nothing on the dark side: liquid-glass-react uses a 1.5px ring, KeyShot a 1px border plus a small offset inset highlight. Comeau adds a 4-6px edge strip with its own blur and brightness to fake thickness. Lensing itself needs an SVG feDisplacementMap used as backdrop-filter: url(), which only Chromium draws. WebKit bug 245510 is still open and its three fix pull requests were unmerged on 2026-11-02, so Safari and Firefox get no lensing.

What to change. Replace the ring stack with: 1px border at .35, one 1.5px masked ring that is bright top-left and fades to zero (the recipe has it), KeyShot-style small inset highlights, one soft inner shade at the bottom, and a layered shadow tinted to the backdrop. Delete the 40px inner glow. Add real lensing only on the corner objects, Chrome and Edge only, with a plain-blur fallback.
Sources: developer.apple.com · github.com · keyshot.com · joshwcomeau.com · kube.io · github.com · bugs.webkit.org
6

The three corner objects read as app-icon tiles, not glass

They are filled 40-66% white, their blur(12px) works on an already-blurred room so it does nothing, and at 89-131px they are small. Refraction is the cue that tells the eye an object is glass, and it is cheapest and most visible on small objects. Figma's own Glass effect lists refraction at the edges, depth and dispersion as its controls, which is the same ingredient list as Apple's.

What to change. Fill .10-.30 white, 160-220px (19vmin), radius 28%, each one straddling the pane's corner so half is sharp in the room and half is blurred under the pane, with a sharp coloured shape behind it. Then, in Chrome and Edge only, give them the SVG lens filter described in recipe_css.
Sources: designcompass.org · kube.io · github.com
7

Fonts: macOS is told to draw the thinnest text, and the type is small and regular weight

The body sets -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased through Vibe's own token. In Chromium on a Mac that value switches off font smoothing and hinting, and Skia's comments say smoothed text is drawn from outlines that CoreGraphics thickens while unsmoothed text is not. On Tom's Mac (macOS 26.6.2, a Philips 221V8LB at about 102 ppi, which macOS treats as 1x) I re-ran a probe: smoothing on draws 13-23% more ink, with no colour fringing. Measured with Figtree in Chrome, antialiased 400 has 0.88 of the ink of default 400, antialiased 500 is about default 400, and default 500 is 1.13x. The CSS has 35 declarations at 14px and 32 at 12px, only 5 at 16px and 1 at 28px, and Figtree's x-height is just 0.500 em (7px at 14px, 6px at 12px). Apple says to avoid light weights and to go larger with thin type. Honest limit: this was measured in headless Chrome on synthetic specimens, nobody has compared the real dashboard with the flag removed, and the glass layer itself did not thin text in my tests.

What to change. Remove antialiased, and override the Vibe token on :root, because Vibe applies it in 62 places. Body 15px/500, titles 18px/600, KPI numbers 40px/700, nothing below 13px except uppercase labels. Ink #0f172a and #1e293b. Details in type_scale.
Sources: chromium.googlesource.com · raw.githubusercontent.com · lists.w3.org · mjtsai.com · developer.apple.com · cdn.jsdelivr.net · startech.com.bd

What has to be behind the glass

What must be behind the glass: something sharp, colourful and varied, because glass is only visible by what it does to something. Targets, measured against today's photo. Hue spread: at least 100 degrees across three families (today one band, 201-220 degrees). Brightness: from a dark area around 25% lightness (a navy shape) to a bright area around 95% (today a narrow pale band, roughly 40-95% of range). Detail: hard edges and shapes between 4 and 40px across, so an 18px blur has something to soften. Shapes: three to five, 220-420px at 1080p, with 40-60% of each shape under the pane and the rest sharp outside it, so the eye sees sharp turn to soft across the same edge. Source resolution at least 2560px wide if it is a photo (today 1664). Do not blur, bloom or grade it in advance; the pane does the softening.

Option A, built in code (my recommendation for the light, pastel look of Tom's second reference and for the reel's bright feel). It costs no download, stays sharp at any resolution, and is the version I rendered and saw working (the audit's strongest result). On .env use: linear-gradient(120deg, #6b7dff 0%, #b48cff 45%, #ff9ec4 75%, #7cd4ff 100%) as the base, plus four radial-gradient blobs on top (pink #ffb3d9 near the top left, sky #8fd3ff near the top right, violet #b9a2ff near the bottom right, mint #7ee8d0 near the bottom left, each about 55-60% wide fading to transparent at 60%). Then add hard-edged shapes as absolutely positioned divs sized in vmin so they scale: a white ring about 30vmin across at the top left corner, a navy (#1b2a6b) rounded square about 26vmin on the right edge, a yellow (#ffd45e) capsule about 34vmin by 13vmin rotated -18 degrees at the lower left, a coral (#ff6e64) circle about 24vmin at the bottom right corner, and a green (#3ddc97) bar about 40vmin by 7vmin tilted 8 degrees along the top edge. Put a small inner highlight on each (a radial-gradient white at 35%) so the blurred part under the pane still reads as volume. Place the three .gobj glass objects on top of the shapes at the pane's corners. With the pane inset at 88px, the pane edges sit at x=88 and x=1832, y=88 and y=992, so shapes must cross those lines.

Option B, a photo (for the dark, warm look of Tom's first reference: lamp light, window, bokeh). It must be sharp: a real photo of a living room with warm lamp points and a cool window, or one generated in FLUX.2 klein through Draw Things at 2560x1440 with the steps raised to 10-12 so the edges are crisp. Do not run it through blur, bloom or a single-colour grade. Export WebP at about quality 85 and keep it under about 400KB. With a dark photo, switch the pane to a dark fill: background rgba(12,14,20,.34), backdrop-filter blur(20px) saturate(1.6), border 1px rgba(255,255,255,.16), white text; bright points under the pane bloom into soft discs, which is what sells it. Warning from LogRocket: dark glass over a too-dark photo is invisible, so the photo needs bright points.

How to check it worked (my own heuristics, not from a source): turn the pane's fill to zero and compare brightness just outside and just inside its edge; the average difference should be at least 8 levels (today 0-13). And (1 minus fill) times the detail the blur removes should come to about 5 levels or more (today 0.28 x 5 = 1.5).

The type

Smoothing (do this first, it is the single biggest text change): delete -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased. Set :root{ --font-smoothing-webkit:auto; --font-smoothing-moz:auto; } and body{ -webkit-font-smoothing:auto; -moz-osx-font-smoothing:auto; } because Vibe applies its token in 62 places, so removing it from body alone is not enough. Do not use text-rendering: geometricPrecision; Blink turns hinting off for it too. Quick proof before committing: in Chrome DevTools add that :root override and compare the page at 1920x1080 with it on and off. If antialiased must stay for some reason, add 100 to every weight and 1px to every size, because antialiased 500 only matches default 400. Note that Josh Comeau's widely copied reset keeps antialiased on purpose; that is a Retina-era choice and this monitor is not Retina.

Sizes and weights for Figtree on a 1920x1080 non-Retina screen (about 102 ppi):

- Page title: 28-32px, weight 700, line-height 1.15, letter-spacing -0.02em. (Tom's first reference uses a 56px headline on a hero; a dashboard needs less.)

- KPI numbers: 40px, weight 700, line-height 1.1, letter-spacing -0.02em, proportional figures. Use tabular-nums only inside tables, because Figtree's tabular '1' leaves a wide gap (a '1111' measures 66px proportional but 100px tabular at 40px).

- Card titles: 18px, weight 600, line-height 24px (second reference: 18-20px semibold).

- Body and table rows: 15px, weight 500, line-height 22px, colour #0f172a.

- Secondary text: 13px, weight 500, line-height 18px, colour #1e293b, no added letter-spacing (Apple's table tightens slightly at 13pt, it does not open). Floor of 13px for anything meant to be read.

- Uppercase labels only: 12px, weight 600, letter-spacing +0.06em, colour #1e293b.

- Never 12px/400 slate text, which is what a lot of the page is today.

Colours: fully opaque ink only, no rgba text. #0f172a for primary, #1e293b for secondary. I replaced #334155 because over a thin pane the audit measured it dropping to 3.7-4.8:1 in the worst spots. Contrast I computed for the recipe (pane about .24 white plus card .60 over the darkest backdrop spot, a navy shape): #0f172a about 9.3:1, #1e293b about 7.7:1. Accent colours do not survive small: over that same dark spot #1d4ed8 gives about 3.5:1 and #15803d about 2.6:1, so use accents only as chip fills, dots or numerals of 24px and up, with dark text beside them. Never put text straight on the bare pane; text lives on .card or .rail.

Font loading: Google Fonts serves one variable Figtree file for 400/500/600/700, so those cost nothing extra. To use in-between weights such as 450 or 550, change the request to family=Figtree:wght@300..900. Optional: Inter 500 for body-size text (x-height .546 against Figtree's .500, so it reads larger at 14-15px) and keep Figtree for titles and numbers.

Keep the text sharp: no will-change: transform on any element that holds text (Drawer.module.css line 26, KatieStage.module.css and MotionStage.module.css all set it, so check the open drawer), end every entrance animation at transform: none, use whole-pixel insets (88px, not percentages), no opacity below 1 on text containers at rest. Check the result on the real 1080p monitor, not a Retina laptop.

The audit of our own stylesheet

Tom is right, and the cause is not the blur setting. The glass has nothing to refract and is too opaque to show it. Real glass reads as glass when four things hold. (1) Something sharp, high-contrast and multi-hue sits behind the pane, and you see it crisp outside the pane and soft inside it, across the same edge. (2) The fill is thin, about 10-45% white, so that thing dominates the pane's colour (web.dev's frosted pane is 30%, NN/g's example is 30% with 25px blur). (3) saturate/brightness lifts the colour so the blur does not go muddy. (4) The edge is lit from one direction, bright on the top-left and shaded on the bottom-right, with a sheen and a soft shadow. Apple describes the real thing as bending and concentrating light, not just scattering it (WWDC25 session 219, https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/). We fail (1) and (2) badly, (3) and (4) partly, and a bug makes the cards worse than a plain fill.

Ranked by how much each explains the verdict:

- Backdrop with nothing in it: the room is pre-blurred, one blue hue, and squeezed into a narrow tonal range.

- Slab and card fills at .72, with the card fill sitting on the slab fill.

- Card backdrop-filter inside the slab's backdrop root, which blurs nothing and whitens the cards.

- A 64px frame of room around a pane that covers 82% of the window.

- A directionless edge.

- Corner objects that read as app-icon tiles.

- Type: `-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased` plus light, small, 12-14px text with no display tier. This one explains the fonts half of the verdict only.

Risks

1. Nothing here has been rendered on Tom's own screen. My renders were in headless Chromium 153/156 with software compositing, the GPU path was never tested, and nobody has compared the real dashboard with the font flag removed. Apply the structure fix, take a 1920x1080 screenshot in Tom's Chrome, and tune the fill (.14-.34) and card (.58-.62) numbers by eye.

2. Expect frosted glass, not Apple's Liquid Glass. Real lensing is Chromium-only, WebKit bug 245510 is open and its fix pull requests (68614, 68613, 69566) were still unmerged on 2026-11-02, so any Safari, iPad or iPhone viewer sees the CSS-only version. Make sure that version looks good on its own, because practice owners and reps will not all use Chrome. I could not verify what browser Tom uses; a 1080p monitor says nothing about it.

3. A WebKit bug (297620, status NEW) stops backdrop-filter from working at all when it uses var() on macOS 14 Safari. The recipe uses literal numbers for that reason. Tom's Mac (macOS 26) is not affected, but other viewers could be.

4. Text on a thinner pane has less contrast. The recipe protects it by putting all text on cards at .58-.62 white, and I computed 7.7:1 or better for #1e293b over the darkest spot, but I only computed it for a few sample backdrop colours, not the final art. Re-measure against the real backdrop. Apple's own guidance is to avoid content crossing glass in steady states, and NN/g recommends simple backdrops for legibility; the shapes-crossing-the-edge look comes from the references, not from a rule.

5. I could not open either Dribbble shot or the Instagram reel. Every figure about them (about a third opaque, 40% white pane, 50-60% cards, 48px radius, 40px numbers, 56px headline) comes from Tom's description, unverified. A skeptic who looked at the reel's saved frames in the repo (docs/research/glassy-reference/ and docs/screenshots/glass-v2/reel-vs-ours-1440.png) read it as a thick, tilted slab with a lit side wall, refractive cubes and a floor reflection on a soft blue office, with near-opaque pale cards. That is a visual read, not a confirmed fact, and the reel's tooling is unknown. If Tom wants that exact look, thickness (side wall, tilt, reflection) may matter more than anything in the recipe, and that is a separate piece of work from this one.

6. SVG refraction maps must match the element size exactly and be rebuilt when it changes; they cost most when the backdrop changes (ours is static); and sharp map edges can look jagged (a low-confidence claim, the source page would not open). Use it on three small objects only. Do not animate blur radius.

7. Keep blurred elements few: one big pane plus three small objects is fine. Do not add glass back to cards. If cards ever need to show the room, that requires a second sibling glass layer, and Apple advises against glass on glass.

8. The 'dark glass' variant (Tom's first reference) can vanish over a dark photo (LogRocket); it needs bright points in the photo.

9. The entrance animation changes from a 900ms opacity-plus-tilt to a 500ms transform-only tilt. If Tom wants a fade, fade .pane-content (cards and text), never .stage or .env.
