# Engineering notes

The full working README from development. The front page is `../README.md`.

## What this is

This is the practice dashboard for Katie, the AI receptionist that SAIDA runs on a practice's
phone line, made by SAIDA. It shows the practice what Katie did today: whether the line is open
or on a call, today's calls, bookings, callbacks waiting for the front desk and confirmation
texts, with each call's transcript in a side drawer. The Leads page (2026-11-06) is the treatment
coordinator's list of people who were quoted and have not followed through, with a drawer to
record what happened (booked, came in, no-show, started, lost, pause, reopen, a note): the one
place the dashboard writes anything. A small drawn Katie is off screen by default
and steps in only occasionally (an urgent callback, the first open of the day, a booking milestone,
the line waking up, or when asked with the top bar's Katie button): she walks or rises to the one
widget that matters, says that widget's own plain sentence, and leaves by herself
(`docs/research/MONDAY-LLAMA-2026-10-13.md`, Part 3). It is a React 18 +
Vite single-page app that reads the backend's `/api/dash/*` endpoints on the same origin and is
served at `/dash/`. The agent brief is `brief/PROMPT.md`, the build spec is `docs/DESIGN.md`, and
the code map for anyone changing it is `docs/CONTRACTS.md`. `brief/fixtures/` are real API shapes
generated by the backend, and `brief/assets/katie/` is her original artwork.

**The skin is glass (Tom, 2026-10-26; second pass "the room" the same day; third pass 2026-11-02: the look is
a setting).** The whole app sits in one frosted glass pane standing in a room, with the room showing on all four
sides. Since the third pass every ingredient of the glass is a per-browser setting edited live on Settings > The
glass: what is behind the pane (the shipped office photo `src/assets/scene.webp`, a clearer office, a dusk office
photo, or a colour field drawn in CSS), the pane's fill, blur, colour boost and radius, the edge (the shipped
bevel, a crisp hairline or a gradient rim lit at two corners, with a feathered edge gradient, a drop shadow, a sheen,
grain, the inner glow and the three corner glass objects as switches, and a Chrome-only refraction at the rim), the
cards' fill, edge, blur and radius, the text size and the font smoothing; the room around the pane is 1 % of the
window on every look and not a control. Four presets: Daylight, the default, is the researched recipe with Tom's
tweak (a colour field, a 26 % pane blurred 14 px, a gradient rim, cards without their own blur, 7 % larger text,
crisp text); Frost is the recipe itself; Dusk is a dark look over the dusk photo; Original is the second pass's
glass. Inside the pane: glass cards, a solid blue pill for the active
page, pastel pill chips and four KPI cards with icon tiles; the content scrolls inside the pane and Katie stands on
its bottom edge. The skin, the Settings page, the grid's gap and one-column rules (container queries), the
zoom-aware scroll forwarding (`src/lib/zoom.ts`) and the Katie director's clock stamping changed: pages, data, copy, Katie's
behaviour, the Demo menu and the 1280 x 800 minimum are as before. The skin spec is `docs/DESIGN-GLASS.md` (section
9 is the shipped state, section 8 the second pass it keeps as a preset; the research behind 9 is
`docs/research/glass-lab-2026-11-02/` and the Katie Glass Lab artifact it names), its reviews are in
`docs/review/`, and its screenshots are `docs/screenshots/final/`. The look store is `src/settings/look.ts`, the
Settings card `src/pages/settings/LookCard.tsx`; the tokens and the backdrop, pane, frame, edge, card, glass object,
menu, tooltip and segmented-pill styles are all in `src/styles/glass.css`, the only file outside `src/katie/` with
literal colors.

The backend is a separate private service: it records every call, serves `/api/dash/*` and the built `dist/` at `/dash/`, and Caddy gates both with one basic-auth pair. It is not part of this repo; everything here runs on the built-in demo data or `scripts/fake-api.mjs`.

## Run it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open http://localhost:5173/dash/?mock=1. Mock mode needs no backend and no credentials; it
serves the fictional fixtures in `src/mock/fixtures/` (Harborline Orthodontics, a demo practice,
"now" fixed at Monday October 12, 6:05 PM New York). The scenario is remembered for the browser
tab, and `?mock=0` turns it off again.

| URL | what it shows |
|---|---|
| `/dash/?mock=1` | the fixtures, then a live replay: one stream event every 2 s for a call of about 38 s, a 6 s pause, then a second call with a booking, then quiet. In a fresh tab Katie walks in once, within about 3 s, to Callbacks waiting (the day's first open), holds its sentence for about 8 s and leaves; the replay's urgent note and booking do not bring her back (urgent spacing, no booking milestone crossed, cooldown) |
| `/dash/?mock=empty` | a day with no calls: empty lists, zero numbers, the line idle; Katie does not appear (nothing to point at) |
| `/dash/?mock=slow` | the fixtures and replay with 1.5 s latency on every request, to see the loading skeletons |
| `/dash/?mock=error` | every request fails: each widget shows its error line, the stream backs off and retries |
| `/dash/?mock=demo` | the closers' demo day (next section): a made-up practice day on today's date, 9:10 PM, with a sample call on demand |

`npm run dev:mock` starts the dev server with mock mode on by default; `npm run dev:demo` starts it
as the demo build (the demo day is the default and mock cannot be turned off).

**Seeing Katie in a demo.** She is occasional by design, so most of the time she is not on screen.
To see her:

- open `/dash/?mock=1` in a **fresh tab** (a new tab, not a reload of one that already showed her).
  In mock mode her caps live in the tab's sessionStorage, so every new demo tab gets the day's
  first-open moment, while real mode remembers it per browser in localStorage;
- or click her **head button** at the right end of the top bar ("Point out what needs attention").
  It has no cap: she walks to the most pressing widget on the page and the widget takes focus;
- in a mock tab, the browser console can force any moment through the caps:
  `__katieStage.resetCaps(); __katieStage.trigger('booking')` (also `'urgent'`, `'first-open'`,
  `'line-wake'`, `'summon'`). `__katieStage` exists only in mock mode.

Any click, scroll, wheel or Escape sends her away at once. Settings > Katie switches between
Occasional and Off; "Katie's corner" picks the side she comes from; Settings > The glass edits the skin for the
browser (DESIGN-GLASS 9.7). Her behaviour is specified in
`docs/research/MONDAY-LLAMA-2026-10-13.md` Part 3 and summarised in `docs/DESIGN.md` 7.4.

## Closers' demo

The demo is what a closer screen-shares to a practice owner. It needs no login, no backend and
no SAIDA system: every number comes from a made-up practice day bundled into the page.

**What it shows.** Harborline Orthodontics (fictional) on **today's real date**, with the clock
pinned to **9:10 PM**, so the day is complete: 35 calls answered (4 in Spanish), 27 appointments booked, 17 new-patient consults, 12 callbacks waiting with 1 urgent, 20
confirmation texts with 19 replies, and 29 days of history behind the 7- and 30-day views. Every
call has a full transcript. One more call, the showpiece, plays "now" only when asked.

**The demo build.** Build the static demo site and host `dist-demo/` anywhere static:

```sh
npm run build:demo        # type-checks, then writes dist-demo/ with VITE_DEMO_DEFAULT=1
```

In that build the demo day is the default and the floor: there is no backend, so `?mock=0` or the
Settings toggle cannot switch it to real mode, and it never requests `/api/dash`. `netlify.toml`
publishes `dist-demo/` with the `/dash/*` rewrite and `noindex` headers. Any ordinary build also
shows the demo with `?mock=demo`.

**The URL.** `https://<demo site>/dash/` on the demo build (no parameter needed), or
`<any build>/dash/?mock=demo`. The URL never carries a token, a name or a number; deep links such
as `/dash/callbacks` work. The demo sticks for the browser tab.

**The Demo menu.** "Demo" at the right of the top bar opens:

| item | key | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Reset the day | R | puts the day back as it was at open: the sample call gone, the drawer closed, Katie's limits cleared |
| Play a sample call | P | a new patient calls "now": the Line tile goes on a call and shows the last thing said, a Live row appears, the booking lands, the texts go out, then hang-up and the numbers move (about 2 minutes) |
| Play at 2x | | the same call in about 65 seconds |
| Bring Katie in | K | Katie walks to what matters on the page and says one sentence |
| Show numbers | | full phone numbers (all fictional, 555-01xx) |

The keys never fire while typing in a field. **Reset between demos** (R), or open a fresh tab: a
fresh tab also replays Katie's first-open moment, which should run before the share starts.

**Sharing.** Share a **1280 × 800 browser window at 100% zoom** (the designed minimum), share the
window rather than the whole screen, and turn off Zoom's "Optimize for video clip".

**The 90-second script** (full beat sheet and closer lines in
`docs/research/dashboard-inspiration-2026-10-21/FINDINGS.md`, section 5a):

1. **0-10 s, Overview**, Katie already gone: "This is a practice's phone today with Katie answering."
2. **10-30 s, Overview**: point at **Appointments booked 27**, then **New-patient consults 17**,
   then the chart's morning bars (four calls an hour at 8 and 9 AM). Ask how many of those
   calls their desk misses.
3. **30-50 s, the Spanish consult**: Calls → **Camila Ortega, 8:02 AM** (Spanish). The drawer's
   Outcome block reads checked the chart, booked a new-patient consult, confirmation text replied
   Yes; scroll two Spanish lines.
4. **50-65 s, Overview → Callbacks waiting**: the top row, **Owen Brandt, Urgent, booked on this
   call**: anything Katie should not handle lands here written up, urgent on top.
5. **65-85 s, live**: with the Overview open, press **P** (or Demo → Play at 2x) and narrate the
   Line tile, the Live row and the booking as they happen; then click the new row to show the
   transcript that just happened. (Calling the real demo line live from the dashboard needs a
   server feed that is not built yet.)
6. **85-90 s**: stop sharing and set the go-live date.

**Regenerating the dataset.** The scenes are hand-written JSON in `docs/demo/scenes/`. After
editing one:

```sh
node scripts/demo-data.mjs              # validate every scene and rebuild src/mock/demo/* and docs/demo/DEMO-DATA.md
node scripts/demo-data.mjs --validate   # validate only
TZ=UTC node scripts/check-lib.mjs       # the materializer still matches the manifest on many dates
```

Never edit `src/mock/demo/*` by hand. The dataset's contract is `src/mock/demo/README.md`, and
`docs/demo/DEMO-DATA.md` lists every call.

## Real API (development)

To work against the real API, create a git-ignored `.env.local` in this folder:

```sh
DASH_USER=...
DASH_PASS=...
# optional, default https://dash-backend.example.com
DASH_API_TARGET=...
```

The backend's owner supplies the password. It is the only credential this project uses, and it never enters the
bundle: the Vite dev server proxies `/api/dash` to `DASH_API_TARGET` and adds the Authorization
header on the server side, so the browser never sees it. Process environment variables win over
`.env.local`. Without the pair nothing is proxied and the dev server prints a hint to use
`?mock=1`. Then open http://localhost:5173/dash/ with no `mock` parameter. If the API answers
401, the top bar says the proxy's sign-in was refused.

`scripts/fake-api.mjs` is a stand-in for the backend server, for testing the real (non-mock) code
path without touching SAIDA's systems. It is a dependency-free Node server that answers
`/api/dash/*` from the same fictional fixtures, with basic auth on every path, real Server-Sent
Events and a replayed live call. Use dummy credentials only:

```sh
FAKE_DASH_USER=demo FAKE_DASH_PASS=not-a-secret node scripts/fake-api.mjs
DASH_API_TARGET=http://127.0.0.1:5190 DASH_USER=demo DASH_PASS=not-a-secret npm run dev
```

| variable | meaning |
|---|---|
| `FAKE_DASH_USER`, `FAKE_DASH_PASS` | required; the basic-auth pair it accepts |
| `FAKE_PORT`, `FAKE_HOST` | default 5190 and 127.0.0.1 (loopback only) |
| `FAKE_DIST` | a built `dist/` to serve at `/dash/`, as the box does (`FAKE_DIST=dist`) |
| `FAKE_EVENT_GAP_MS`, `FAKE_REPLAY_DELAY_MS`, `FAKE_PING_MS` | default 2000, 3000 and 15000 |
| `FAKE_REPLAY` | `0` turns the replayed call off (the stream only pings) |
| `FAKE_SHIFT_DAYS` | `auto` (default) moves the fixtures to today in the practice's timezone; `0` keeps their dates. Just after midnight, `auto` can put today's calls later than "now" |
| `FAKE_LATENCY_MS` | added to every JSON answer, default 0 |

With `FAKE_DIST=dist` after a build, http://127.0.0.1:5190/dash/ is the production topology: sign
in with the dummy pair in the browser's prompt.

It also answers the three lead routes (`GET /api/dash/leads`, `GET /api/dash/leads/:id` and
`POST /api/dash/leads/:id/action`, the only request it accepts that is not a GET) from
`src/mock/leadStore.ts`, the same in-memory lead record the mocks use, seeded from
`leads-week.json` and moved onto today. A tap there changes the record until the server restarts.
The lead fixtures (`src/mock/fixtures/leads.json`, `leads-week.json` and
`scripts/fixtures/leads-actions.json`) are written by `python3 scripts/leads-fixtures.py`, which
runs the real backend's lead code on an invented practice (it imports `voice-agent/` read-only and
writes nothing there); run it again when the backend's lead rules change, never edit the files by
hand.

## Build

```sh
npm run build
```

This type-checks and writes `dist/`. The build works from `/dash/` and reads no environment
variables at runtime: the API base is the constant `/api/dash` on the same origin, and the
browser's basic-auth session carries the credentials. A production build still honours
`?mock=1` (and the other scenarios, `?mock=demo` included) for demos; the top bar then says "Demo
data" (the Demo menu for `?mock=demo`), and Settings has a toggle to turn it off for the tab.
`npm run preview` serves `dist/` locally at `/dash/`.

`npm run build:demo` writes the closers' static demo site to `dist-demo/` (git-ignored; see
"Closers' demo"). It is a separate deliverable and never goes to the box.

## Deploy

`dist/` is the whole deliverable. It is copied to the backend server, where Caddy gates `/dash*` and
`/api/dash*` with basic auth and proxies both to the backend app, which serves the files. Never copy
`dist-demo/` there. Nothing in `dist/` holds a secret. Source maps are not written unless
`DASH_SOURCEMAP=1` is set, and they are never deployed.

## Sign-in

Two modes, chosen by the box, never by the build. On start the app reads the public
`GET /api/dash/config` (`{auth: "basic" | "cognito", idle_minutes, remember_days, support_email}`,
and for cognito also `user_pool_id`, `client_id` and `region`). A missing endpoint (an older box) or a
network error reads as **basic**.

- **basic** (today's box and the reps' shared login): the browser's basic-auth session, no sign-in
  screens, no account line, no People or Activity cards. Nothing changes.
- **cognito** (a practice's box): the app signs people in itself, on the glass screens in
  `src/auth/screens/`, with Amplify Auth v6 (SRP, `aws-amplify/auth`). Screens: sign in (email +
  password), choose a password (the first sign-in uses a temporary one), choose a code (authenticator
  app or email), set up the app (QR + the secret as copyable text + the first code), the six-digit
  code (with "Remember this computer for N days", N from `remember_days`, and "Use my email code
  instead" when both are set up), the idle lock (password only) and forgot password (text only: ask
  the practice owner or SAIDA support at `support_email`; there is no self-serve reset). Errors are
  plain words, never an exclamation mark, and no screen shows patient data.
  Every API call then carries `Authorization: Bearer <access token>`; the live stream reads
  `text/event-stream` with `fetch` and the same header (`src/live/sse.ts`), because `EventSource` cannot
  send one. A 401 (`{"error": "sign in" | "expired" | "signed out"}`) returns to the sign-in screen on
  the same route; a 403 shows "Not allowed.". After the code step with the box ticked the app calls
  `rememberDevice()` and stores the date in localStorage `katie.auth.rememberedAt`; past `remember_days`
  it calls `forgetDevice()` and clears the date, so the next sign-in asks for a code again. After
  `idle_minutes` without a pointer, key or scroll event the app empties the query cache, unmounts the
  pages and shows the lock screen; unlocking is a fresh password sign-in (a remembered computer gets no
  code). Sign out posts `POST /api/dash/session` and signs out. The top bar says "Signed in as <name>".
  Owners and SAIDA support get two Settings cards (People: add, remove, reset password, reset code, send
  the invite again, and the SAIDA support switch that only an owner can flip; Activity: the last 30 days
  in plain sentences, filtered by person). Staff see neither.

Try it without a backend: `?mock=1&auth=1` (sticks to the tab; `?auth=0` turns it off) shows the
cognito-mode screens on invented people. Every code is `000000`; any password signs in except the
literal `wrong-password`; `owner@harborline.example` is the owner, `staff@harborline.example` has both
code kinds, `frontdesk@harborline.example` has the email code, and `newperson@harborline.example`
signs in with `temporary-1` and then chooses a password. Nothing is a credential. The same people
drive the verifier's `auth` step. A real cognito box cannot be exercised from here (no AWS).

## Privacy

Phone numbers are masked by default (`(656) ***-0150`). The Show numbers toggle adds `reveal=1` to
the API requests that carry numbers; the toggle's on/off state is remembered in this browser, but
the revealed numbers are never stored, logged or put in the page URL, and turning the toggle off
removes them from memory at once. No patient information goes into a URL, the tab title or the
console: titles are only "Calls · Katie" and the like. The app makes same-origin requests only,
plus Google Fonts for the Figtree typeface, which carries no patient data.

In cognito mode the sign-in talks to Amazon Cognito (the email, the password and the codes: no patient
data). The access token lives in memory (Amplify); Amplify keeps the refresh token and the device key in
localStorage, which "Remember this computer" needs (accepted). A token, a password or a code is never put
in a URL, a title, a log or a storage key of ours (the only keys are `katie.auth.rememberedAt`, a date,
and, in mock mode, `katie.mockauth`). The Content-Security-Policy that lets the page reach Cognito is
Caddy's (`connect-src 'self' https://cognito-idp.<region>.amazonaws.com`); this repo ships none.

## How Katie's rig is organised

Katie is one inline SVG (`src/katie/Katie.tsx`, viewBox 652 x 1544, feet on the bottom edge,
96 px tall on the page) built from two raster layers and a set of drawn parts. The rasters are the
head (hair, face, ears, neck) and the torso (the scrub top with the arms erased); they are
regenerated by `node scripts/katie-assets.mjs` from `brief/assets/katie/` into `src/katie/assets/`,
and the script fails unless every opaque pixel is byte-identical to the artwork. Everything else
is drawn SVG built once at load: the path data for each part (sleeves, upper arms, forearms,
hands, lower top, pelvis, thighs, shins, sneakers, eyelids, the torso side strokes) is in
`src/katie/parts.ts`, using the small shape helpers in `geometry.ts`; the sleeve shading and eye
shapes in `traced.ts` are generated by `docs/katie-art/trace-shading.mjs` and are not edited by
hand; measurements, widths and the palette are in `rig.ts`; and the pose type, the joint pivots
and the preset poses are in `pose.ts`. The pivots come from `docs/katie-art/rig.json` (neck
322, 352; shoulders 170, 440 and 482, 440; elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles below them).
Layers draw back to front: far leg, near leg, pelvis, lower top, torso raster, torso side
strokes, the screen-left arm, the head with its eyelids, then the screen-right arm, which always
draws above the head so the listening hand covers the ponytail. To swap a part, edit its path in
`parts.ts` (or its numbers in `rig.ts`), keep it in the same group and keep that group's pivot,
then check it on `/dash/_katie` in the dev server, where a slider moves each joint, a button runs
each state and a 50 % master-art overlay shows any drift from the original drawing. Motion lives in
`src/katie/motion/` (the walk cycle, hop arcs, path planning, and every duration, ease and pose
angle in `timing.ts`), and the choreography on the dashboard lives in `src/katie/stage/`: `moments.ts` decides when she
may appear (ranks, cooldown, daily cap, urgent spacing, quiet-moment waits; pure and unit-tested in
`scripts/check-lib.mjs`), `director.ts` runs one appearance (travel or rise, hold, exit, dismissal),
and `placement.ts` / `fitBubble.ts` place her and her bubble.
`docs/KATIE-ART.md` explains how every number was measured.
